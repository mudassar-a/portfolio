About

I work on machine learning for physical models. My published first-author paper (Chinese Physics C) fuses large theoretical model grids with sparse precision measurements through transfer learning and Bayesian-optimised regression. A second study, under review in Astronomy and Computing, benchmarks whether neural summaries and estimators extract known cosmological information with calibrated uncertainties.

I completed my M.S. in Physics at the Institute of Space Technology, Islamabad, in April 2025 with Cum Laude distinction (CGPA 3.63/4.00). My interests sit where imperfect theory meets scarce data: characterising model systematics, validating inference pipelines end to end, and reporting what a method can and cannot do.

Research

Estimator efficiency in simulation-based inference from large-scale structure under review

Independent project (CosmoWebSBI), 2026 — Astronomy and Computing

A controlled benchmark of 14,400 log-normal large-scale-structure mocks over (Ωm, σ8, b). Neural posterior estimation from the power spectrum, counts-in-cells and a 3D-CNN field embedding, validated with simulation-based calibration, coverage tests, and Fisher and MCMC baselines. Posteriors recovered Ωm to about 2% of the prior width; combining summaries added only 3.6% in figure of merit, reported as a near-null result.

Machine learning for nuclear charge radii published

M.S. thesis, 2024–2025 — Chinese Physics C 50(2), 024109

A transfer-learning neural network pre-trained on 7,517 relativistic mean-field calculations and fine-tuned on 1,017 measured radii, cutting root-mean-square error by 51.7% against previously published networks, alongside a Bayesian-optimised CatBoost regressor (test RMSE 0.0090 fm). Both models reproduce shell closures and deformation trends.
